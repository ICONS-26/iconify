Common Interview Questions

Draft your own answer first, then tap "Show Answer" to compare against a model response.

Q1. Tell me about yourself.

Easy
Model approach: Structure it as Present → Past → Future: your current course/role, one or two relevant achievements or projects, and what you're looking for next. Keep it under 90 seconds and tie it back to the job you're interviewing for.

Q2. What are your strengths and weaknesses?

Medium
Model approach: Pick a strength relevant to the role and back it with a quick example. For weaknesses, choose something real but not disqualifying, and show what you're actively doing to improve it.

Q3. Why should we hire you?

Medium
Model approach: Match 2-3 of your skills or experiences directly to what the role needs, and mention something specific about the company that shows you've done your research.

Q4. Describe a time you faced a conflict in a team and how you resolved it.

Hard
Model approach: Use the STAR method — Situation, Task, Action, Result. Focus most of your answer on the Action (what you specifically did) and end with a concrete, positive Result.

Q5. Where do you see yourself in five years?

Easy
Model approach: Show ambition that's realistic and aligned with the company's growth paths — e.g. growing technically, taking on more ownership — rather than a vague or unrelated goal.

Quick Tips

In a GD, quality of points matters more than talk time — aim to speak 3-4 times with substance rather than dominating the conversation. In interviews, pause before answering rather than filling silence with "um" — a two-second pause reads as confidence, not hesitation. Record yourself doing a mock answer at least once; most people are surprised by their own filler words and pace.
